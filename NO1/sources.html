Technical appendix

Transit time, arrival time and arterial route

PTT is travel time for a pressure wave between arterial sites. PWV = arterial path length / PTT. Wave propagation is distinct from blood-cell velocity. Comparable waveform upstrokes are needed; changes in shape and reflections can alter peak timing. [1]

An ECG event precedes mechanical ejection. With a consistent timing convention, PAT = PEP + PTT, where PEP is pre-ejection period. Thus ECG-to-peripheral-pulse time is not pure vascular transit. The cited comparison used an animal experiment; it is not human wearable-performance validation. [2]

The ear and finger are on separate arterial branches. Their relative arrival times are differences of travel times from a common origin, not direct travel along an ear-to-finger vessel. A wrist–finger experiment needs appropriate same-side placement, a defensible arterial route and a path estimate. Straight-line separation is not automatically arterial path length. This geometric distinction is part of the proposed experiment’s assumptions.

Segmental stiffness is not central aortic stiffness

Peripheral wrist–finger timing characterizes the selected peripheral segment. It cannot be relabeled as a central aortic measurement. Higher PWV can reflect greater effective stiffness under comparable conditions, but pressure, muscle tone, wall thickness, diameter and measurement site also matter. PWV alone does not isolate intrinsic material elasticity or Young’s modulus. [3] [4]

Synchronization is one part of the uncertainty

A useful local-to-shared clock model is t_shared = a × t_local + b: b corrects offset and a corrects rate mismatch. Both need checking over time. FTSP provides a primary example of timestamp placement and drift compensation in wireless sensor networks; SharkTooth addresses BLE body-sensor synchronization. Their results apply to their own implementations, not automatically to this platform. [7] [8]

Sensor acquisition timing, sampling interval, oscillator drift, timestamp generation, FIFO buffering, filter delay, packet loss, attachment pressure, motion and waveform detection contribute to end-to-end uncertainty. At 100 Hz, samples are 10 ms apart. Sub-sample estimation can refine a waveform feature under suitable signal assumptions, but timestamp units and interpolation are not guarantees of timing accuracy. [1]

Cuffless blood pressure needs a separate test

PTT and PWV can inform BP-estimation research, but there is no universal conversion from this demo’s wave speed to mmHg. A BP algorithm needs its own model, calibration strategy, independent reference measurements and validation across intended users, conditions and time since calibration. A large smartwatch study compared calibration-based and calibration-free approaches and found materially different performance across populations. It does not validate SensWear or the hypothetical network. [9]

Consumer access and current SensWear capability

The ring, watch and earbud scenario is conditional on suitable sensors, raw waveforms and trustworthy acquisition times. No support is claimed for synchronizing arbitrary consumer brands or extracting physiological signals from every AirPod or earbud. The ear example is supported by a research earpiece. [5]

The current SensWear Python SDK documents microsecond timestamp fields for IMU/touch, millisecond interpolated timestamps for PPG, and effective PPG acquisition at 200 samples/s/channel. Its Current Time Service sets whole-second UTC values; fractions256 currently reports/writes zero. These interfaces do not demonstrate millisecond alignment between devices. The platform is a research and prototyping tool, and precise network synchronization is the proposed next experiment. [11] [10]

The demos are synthetic

No participant recordings, clinical data or measured SensWear performance are plotted. The SVG waveforms and transport diagram are original teaching graphics generated locally by deck.js.

Clock-offset model

Assume an arterial path of 0.20 m and true transit of 40 ms. A positive offset means the finger clock labels events too late.

v_est = 0.20 / ((40 + offset_ms) / 1000)

The slider ranges from −20 to +20 ms. The plot uses identical waveform shapes separated by a controlled delay. Noise, morphology changes, filtering errors and path uncertainty are omitted to isolate the clock effect. The 5.0 m/s value is not a health threshold.

Relative-stiffness model

v(k) = 5√k m/s   ·   PTT(k) = 200 / v(k) ms

k is an assumed effective wall-stiffness ratio. At k = 1, speed is 5.0 m/s and transit is 40.0 ms. At k = 2.25, they are 7.5 m/s and 26.7 ms. At k = 4, speed doubles to 10.0 m/s and transit halves to 20.0 ms.

The square-root dependence follows ideal Moens–Korteweg scaling with geometry and fluid density fixed; the demo also holds operating pressure fixed. The animated wall expansion is a schematic visual cue, not a simulated pressure–diameter solution. Motion is slowed 50×. Real arteries are nonlinear and this simplified model is not fitted to a person. [3] [4]
